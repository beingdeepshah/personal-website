About Me

I am a hydrologist and water resources researcher with research interests spanning remote sensing, drought monitoring, reservoir dynamics, hydrological modeling, climate change, and water security.

My research uses satellite observations, hydrological models, and data-driven methods to understand how natural climate variability and human activities influence freshwater systems.

I received my Ph.D. in Civil and Water Resources Engineering from Texas A&M University and currently work as a Postdoctoral Researcher at Texas A&M University.

Research

My research focuses on understanding freshwater availability and hydrological extremes across regional and global scales.

Reservoirs from Space

Using multi-satellite observations to monitor global lakes and reservoirs, including storage, surface area, and evaporation.

Drought & Extremes

Understanding drought propagation, reservoir vulnerability, recovery, and the effects of human water management.

Hydrological Forecasting

Developing data-driven and hydrological forecasting approaches for reservoir storage and water-resource outlooks.

Climate & Water Security

Investigating how climate variability and anthropogenic pressures affect freshwater availability and long-term water security.

Selected Projects

NASA Global Water Reservoir Product Suite

Contributed to the development and validation of global reservoir monitoring products using MODIS and VIIRS satellite observations.

Global Reservoir Drought Monitoring

Developed approaches for evaluating reservoir-based hydrological droughts using storage and evaporation information.

Drought Propagation to Reservoirs

Investigating how meteorological droughts propagate into reservoir droughts and identifying differences in preparation, response, and recovery times across global reservoirs.

Reservoir Storage Forecasting

Developing reservoir storage forecasting outlooks using hydrological information and machine-learning approaches.

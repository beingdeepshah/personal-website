About

About Me

I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University of Colorado Boulder, working with Dr. Balaji Rajagopalan, Dr. Edith Zagona, and Dr. Joseph Kasprzyk.

My current research focuses on evaluating how improvements in streamflow forecasts translate into better water-management decisions in the Colorado River Basin. I use RiverWare and the Bureau of Reclamation’s Colorado River Mid-term Modeling System (CRMMS) to investigate how improved forecasts can support reservoir operations under complex planning policies and a range of forecast scenarios.

My Ph.D. research focused on using MODIS and VIIRS satellite observations to characterize the area, elevation, storage, and evaporation of large reservoirs worldwide. I used these observations to investigate reservoir droughts and water shortages, including their propagation and recovery times, severity, and the extent to which they are driven by climate variability versus human water management.

Research

Research Themes

01

Monitoring Reservoirs from Space

Using multi-satellite Earth observations to monitor lake and reservoir surface area, elevation storage, evaporation, bathymetry, and long-term freshwater dynamics.

02

Reservoir Based Droughts/Shortages

Developing reservoir-based drought indicators and investigating reservoir vulnerability, drought propagation, water-shortage severity, and recovery, including the roles of climate variability and human water management.

03

Colorado River Operations

Evaluating how streamflow forecast skill affects reservoir operations, pool elevations, water allocation, and system-wide water-management decisions.

04

ML based Forecasting

Developing machine-learning models to forecast streamflow and reservoir storage.

05

Climate & Water Resources

Investigating how climate variability and climate change influence water availability, droughts, groundwater, and water stress across river basins in the Indian subcontinent.

06

Human–climate Interactions

Understanding how irrigation, reservoir operations, groundwater pumping, and other human activities alter flood potential and hydrological variability.

Selected Research Projects

Bureau of Reclamation & NOAA

How Do Improvements in Streamflow Forecasts Translate into Better Water Management Decisions in the Colorado River Basin?

Applying RiverWare and the Colorado River Mid-term Modeling System (CRMMS) to evaluate operational projections, water-allocation rules, and alternative streamflow forecast scenarios.

NASA

Drought Propagation from Meteorological to Reservoir Droughts at a Global Scale

Developed a reservoir-specific drought propagation framework to quantify preparation and response times and identify reservoirs with rapid drought propagation and prolonged recovery.

NASA

NASA Global Water Reservoir Product Suite

Contributed to the development of a global reservoir dataset using MODIS and VIIRS observations and investigated the contribution of surface-water storage dynamics to total water-storage variability.

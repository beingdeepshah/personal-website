About

About Me

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University of Colorado Boulder working at the intersection of hydrology, water-resources systems, remote sensing, and hydroclimatic extremes.

My research focuses on understanding how reservoirs respond to drought, climate variability, human water use, and changing hydrological conditions. I use satellite observations, hydrological and reservoir-system models, and data-driven techniques to analyze freshwater systems from basin to global scales.

My current research includes evaluating how improvements in streamflow forecasts translate into better water-management decisions in the Colorado River Basin. I use RiverWare and the Colorado River Mid-term Modeling System to investigate reservoir operations, pool elevations, water-allocation policies, and forecast scenarios.

My broader research has focused on global reservoir monitoring using MODIS and VIIRS observations, reservoir-based drought monitoring, drought propagation, groundwater and flood variability, and the impacts of climate change on water resources.

Research

Research Themes

My work spans satellite hydrology, drought science, reservoir operations, forecasting, and water-resources decision making.

01

Reservoirs from Space

Using multi-satellite Earth observations to monitor lake and reservoir surface area, storage, evaporation, bathymetry, and long-term changes in freshwater availability.

02

Reservoir Droughts

Developing reservoir-based drought indicators and understanding reservoir vulnerability, drought propagation, preparation time, response time, and recovery.

03

Colorado River Operations

Evaluating how streamflow forecast skill affects reservoir operations, water allocation, pool elevations, and system-wide decision making across the Colorado River Basin.

04

Hydrological Forecasting

Developing and evaluating forecast systems for reservoir storage, streamflow, and hydrological conditions using models and machine-learning approaches.

05

Climate & Water Resources

Investigating the effects of climate variability and climate change on water availability, droughts, floods, groundwater, and water stress.

06

Human–Water Interactions

Understanding how irrigation, reservoir operations, groundwater use, and other human activities alter hydrological variability and freshwater availability.

Projects

Selected Research Projects

Bureau of Reclamation & NOAA

How Do Improvements in Streamflow Forecasts Translate into Better Water Management Decisions in the Colorado River Basin?

Applying RiverWare and the Colorado River Mid-term Modeling System (CRMMS) to evaluate reservoir operations, water-allocation rules, pool elevations, and alternative streamflow forecast scenarios.

NASA

Drought Propagation from Meteorological to Reservoir Droughts at a Global Scale

Developed a reservoir-specific drought propagation framework to quantify preparation and response times and identify reservoirs with rapid drought propagation and prolonged recovery.

NASA

NASA Global Water Reservoir Product Suite

Contributed to the development of a global reservoir dataset using MODIS and VIIRS observations and investigated the contribution of surface-water storage dynamics to total water-storage variability.

NASA

Reservoir-Based Drought Monitoring System

Developed a global reservoir drought-monitoring framework combining storage and evaporation observations and investigated natural and human influences on freshwater availability.

Government of India

Drought Onset and Termination in India

Developed an Integrated Drought Index for monitoring meteorological, agricultural, and hydrological droughts and supporting drought detection and early warning.

Government of India

Climate Change Impacts on Water Resources of the Sabarmati Basin

Assessed future water availability, water stress, drivers, and uncertainties using global climate-model projections.
